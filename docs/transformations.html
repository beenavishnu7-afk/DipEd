Transformations 1

This report describes my collaboration with teachers from the Business and Physical Education departments to plan an integrated lesson for a Form 4 CSEC Information Technology class. The lesson was developed to help students apply spreadsheet skills to a practical activity using an interdisciplinary approach to curriculum integration.

As the Information Technology teacher, I asked the Business teacher and the Physical Education teacher at my school how their subject areas can be integrated into a lesson using Spreadsheet formulae and functions. This started a discussion of an approach that included the organization of a school Sports Day as the context of the lesson.

The Physical Education teacher suggested that students could identify some sporting activities, such as relay, sprints and fun activities and determine the equipment required. These items can include balls, batons, cones, and whistles. Students can enter the item, quantity needed and cost.

The Business teacher recommended preparing a budget. They can estimate revenue from ticket sales, identify other areas of revenue, list expenses and by using formulas and functions they can determine whether a profit can be made or a loss. Additionally, they can determine ways to increase revenue or decrease costs after completing the budget.

As the Information Technology teacher, I included that students use spreadsheet functions and formula to calculate expenditure and income as well as profit or loss. A lesson plan was then created according to these suggestions along with the details supplied by both teachers.

At the beginning of the lesson, I introduced a scenario in which the school needed to organize a Sports Day with limited budget. Students were asked to consider the equipment required and how the school could determine whether it has sufficient funds to organize the event.

The class was then asked to work in groups to create a budget. They were provided with a handout of an example of a budget provided by the business teacher and a list of equipment and its cost needed for the sports day. Students then determined cost for a ticket to calculate revenue. They were then instructed to create the budget.

An observation checklist was used as the assessment to determine the correct entry of equipment items and their related data, accurate use of multiplication formula, correct use of the sum function, accurate revenue and profit or loss calculation, appropriate formatting and organization and sensible cost-saving and revenue increasing recommendations.

An integrated curriculum can be within disciplines, across disciplines or real-life issues as explained by models such as intradisciplinary, interdisciplinary, multidisciplinary, and transdisciplinary. Intradisciplinary can be described as the integration of subdisciplines within a subject area. Interdisciplinary is curriculum organized around common learnings across disciplines. Multidisciplinary addresses each separate discipline around a common theme. Transdisciplinary promotes learning by the development of life skills within real-life contexts.

Jacobs (1989) discusses interdisciplinary curriculum design as a way of connecting disciplines through meaningful themes and problems. The interdisciplinary model of curriculum integration was used in this lesson. This model connects concepts and skills from different subject areas, namely business and Physical Education, around a common learning outcome. The integration of IT, Business and Physical Education in this lesson supports this design because each subject contributes knowledge and skills needed to complete the budget. Through this connection, students can apply knowledge from several disciplines to a common task.

The common theme in this lesson was planning a Sports Day budget. Information Technology provided spreadsheet tools for organizing the data and performing the calculations. Business provided the concepts of budgeting, revenue, expenditure and profit or loss and Physical Education provided the sporting context, including activities and equipment requirements.

The three subjects worked together to support a common outcome: producing a realistic and financially responsible Sports Day budget. Students had to draw on knowledge from all three disciplines to complete the task successfully.

This collaborative approach provided several benefits for teaching and learning. First, it allowed me to draw on the expertise of my colleagues to develop a lesson that was meaningful. Second, it strengthened the professional relationship among us as teachers. Third, it allowed for the creative enactment of the curriculum.

The lesson also encouraged student-centered learning, it enabled transfer of knowledge and skills and deepened the connections across different fields of knowledge. Students were required to enter data, perform calculations, discuss options, and make recommendations. They could appreciate how spreadsheets are used in everyday situations and in the world of work. The activity also promoted problem-solving skills, teamwork, communication, digital literacy, and financial decision-making.

One challenge experienced in this lesson was the allocation of time. Students required more time to complete the task. Another challenge was incorporating differentiation. Some groups required additional support with the formula and functions to perform the calculations. While other groups needed ideas for increasing revenue and decreasing cost. In future, I would provide a partially completed spreadsheet template for students requiring additional guidance and provide a list of possible ideas for increasing revenue and decreasing cost.

In conclusion, collaborating with teachers from the business and Physical Education departments provided an opportunity to plan a meaningful interdisciplinary lesson for the Form 4 CSEC Information Technology students. By using the planning of a Sports Day as the common theme, we connected spreadsheet formulas and functions with budgeting, financial decision-making and sports equipment and costs.

The interdisciplinary model enabled students to apply knowledge from three subject areas to solve a practical problem. The lesson also promoted collaboration, student-centered learning, and the development of transferable skills.

I intend to continue working with colleagues across different departments to strengthen the connections between subjects so that students can apply their learning to real-life situations.

References

Jacobs, H. H. (Ed.). (1989). Interdisciplinary curriculum: Design and implementation. Association for Supervision and Curriculum Development.

Transformations 2

Question 2(a)

In education, change agent is someone who can transform teaching and learning, as well as school operations. My motivation to become a change agent comes from within me, through my passion for teaching and desire for continuous improvement, as well as from my students and the changing demands of society.

I have always believed that teaching is more than imparting knowledge. It is about making a difference in students’ lives. This motivates me to explore better ways of reaching them. I understand that students learn differently and have varied abilities. Therefore, I am willing to adapt my methods to meet students where they are before guiding them towards greater achievement. I believe that being a change agent begins with being willing to change myself.

My students also motivate me to be a change agent to advocate for fairness and inclusion. Every student should be treated fairly, regardless of their background. They should be able to learn in a safe, caring and inclusive environment where they can feel respected, valued and comfortable expressing themselves. I believe that a caring classroom can positively influence both teaching and learning.

Furthermore, I am also motivated to prepare students for a changing world. By integrating technology and developing critical thinking, communication, problem-solving and collaboration skills, I can help them become responsible citizens.

Question 2(b)

Through what I have learnt so far in the Postgraduate Diploma in Education programme, I recognize that I can influence teaching and learning by creating a healthy school environment, planning purposeful lessons, using authentic assessments, integrating technology and collaborating with others.

I am making a conscious effort to promote a healthy school environment where students feel safe, respected, valued and comfortable expressing themselves. I believe that positive teacher-student relationships, fairness, empathy, and mutual respect, contribute to students’ emotional well-being and academic success.

I strive to plan lessons with clear objectives and activities that connect classroom learning to real-life situations. This helps students understand the relevance of what they learn and apply their knowledge to everyday challenges. I also recognize the importance of authentic assessments, such as projects, presentations, and practical tasks, which allow students to demonstrate their understanding and develop essential skills.

Furthermore, I also aim to integrate technology meaningfully into my lessons to increase student engagement and support different learning needs. Technology can make abstract concepts easier to understand and provide opportunities for interactive and independent learning.

I am also committed to collaborating with colleagues, sharing ideas, reflecting on my teaching practices, and learning from feedback. Through these efforts, I hope to contribute to a positive school culture, improve teaching and learning and become a more effective change agent in education.

Lesson plan: Spreadsheet formula and functions (Sports Day)

Lesson Plan

Teacher Name:

Beena Seepersad Vishnu

Subject:

Information Technology

Class Level:

Form 4

Unit:

Spreadsheet

Lesson:

Formula and Functions

Date:

22/10/2026

  1. Prior knowledge/skills

What are the steps to

  • Open a spreadsheet?

  • Create a worksheet?

  • Enter, edit and format data in cells?

Identify row, column and cell reference.

What is the symbol for multiplying in spreadsheet?

  1. Lesson objectives :

At the end of this lesson students should be able to:

1) Identify at least four items of equipment needed for a School Sport Day with at least 80% accuracy.

(cognitive, remember)

2) Manipulate data in a spreadsheet with at least 80% accuracy. (cognitive, remember)

3) Apply formula and function to calculate item cost and total expenses and revenue. (cognitive, apply)

4) Format a spreadsheet budget with appropriate headings, numerical entries, formulas and currency formatting. (psychomotor)

5) Collaborate respectfully, demonstrate responsible financial decision-making (Affective)

  1. Set induction

Present a scenario: Our school is preparing for its annual sports day. The principal has asked our class to help organize the event, but the school has a limited budget. How can we decide what to buy, calculate the cost and determine whether the money available will be enough. Ask students to suggest what the school might need to purchase. Ask for answers from Physical education students in class. Explain that the students will become the school sports day planning team and must use a spreadsheet to prepare the budget.

  1. Resources including digital resources

  • Laptop computers

  • Smart tv

  • Prepared worksheet with sample prices

  • Whiteboard and markers

  • Sports equipment price list

  • Assessment checklist

  1. Content/ Teaching Points

1) Present Sports Day Scenario and ask introductory questions.

2) Explain budget, revenue, expenses and profit and loss

3) Demonstrate spreadsheet formula and formatting

4) Distribute the budget template and guide students through entering data and calculating item costs

5) Assign the Sports Day budget activity

6) Circulate to provide guidance

7) Checks answers asks review questions

  1. Teaching strategies

Problem-based learning, collaboration, teacher modelling and demonstration, questioning and guided discovery, curriculum integration

Differentiated Instruction

Contingency plan

  1. Student Activities

1) Identify sporting equipment and suggest possible expenses.

2)Answer questions, observe demonstration and identify the correct formulas and functions.

3) Create the budget using Microsoft Excel by entering data, formula and functions

4) Recommend cost saving measure or increasing income method

  1. Sectional review/ Assessment

Assessment Rubric

Award points as follows:

Assessment Criterion Marks
Correct entry of at least five equipment items and related data 5
Accurate use of multiplication Formulas 5
Correct use of SUM function 5
Accurate revenue and profit/loss calculation 5
Appropriate formatting and organization 3
Sensible cost saving recommendation 2
Total 25

  1. Closure

Ask students to complete the following statement:

“Today I learnt that spreadsheets can help a school organize Sports Day by….”

Ask two students to share their responses.

Spreadsheets are useful tools for organizing data, calculating costs, analyzing financial information and making decisions.
