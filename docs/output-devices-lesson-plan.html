Output Devices lesson plan

The full lesson plan, with objectives, phases of the lesson, teaching strategies and assessment.

Output Devices

Lesson Plan

Learning Objectives:

By the end of the lesson, given an assigned output device, advertisement prompts, and 15 minutes of preparation time, students will be able to:

1) Accurately identify at least four common output devices. (Remember)

2) Describe the purpose of an assigned output device by stating at least one correct function. (Understand)

3) Explain at least two appropriate uses of the assigned output device. (Understand)

4) Apply their knowledge to create an advertisement. (Apply)

5) Create a 30-45 second that includes the device’s name, purpose, two uses and one advantage. (Create)

6) Justify which advertisement they believe was the most effective based on the agreed criteria. (Evaluate)

7) Demonstrate teamwork and respectful communication by contributing to the group task and interacting appropriately with group members and classmates throughout the activity.
Previous Knowledge/Skills

Students should be able to:

  • Explain what an advertisement is and what makes it effective.

  • Identify common computer devices from pictures.

  • Work cooperatively in small groups.
Resources

  • Royal Castle advertisement/ jingle video

  • Projector

  • Laptop

  • Output Devices lesson notes

  • Advertisement planning sheet

  • Colored paper and markers

  • Pencils/ colored pencils

  • Peer advertisement scoring rubric

  • Whiteboard and whiteboard markers
Phase Teacher Activity Student Expected Activity Teaching Strategy
Introduction Introduce myself: Good day everyone, my name is Beena Seepersad-Vishnu. I am here as part of the Information Technology cohort in the Post Graduate Diploma in Education program. I will facilitate your learning of OUTPUT DEVICES, their purpose, characteristics and uses. Listen to introduction. Creating a healthy classroom environment
Set induction

Play a short Royal Castle advertisement/jingle.

Ask students:

  • What caught your attention?

  • What made you remember the advertisement?

  • What did the jingle make you want to do?

  • What makes an advertisement effective?

Write responses on the whiteboard such as catchy phrases, clear message, information about the product, creativity, knowing the target audience, attractive presentation

Watch and listen to the advertisement and respond to guiding questions.

Identify features that make an advertisement memorable and persuasive.

 Questioning, discussion, activating prior knowledge
Knowledge check

Display Cowen Hamilton Computer Company’s products of output devices.

Ask: “Which devices do you recognize”

“What do these devices have in common?”

 Respond to questions and share prior knowledge. Inquiry-based questioning, formative assessment
Reveal the Challenge Tell students: Today you are no longer just students. You are an advertising team competing to be hired by Cowen Hamilton Computer company to promote its output devices/products! Explain that each group will receive an output device and must create an advertisement for it. Introduce the possible formats: jingle, poster, skit, rap, any other suitable idea. Listen to the challenge and ask/answer questions about the task. Students form groups of 3-4 and assign roles Gamification, cooperative learning
Advertisement Challenge Give each group an output-device card and an Advertisement Challenge Card. Explain the scoring rubric. Circulate, question group and provide clarification. Work collaboratively to research lesson notes and create an advertisement. Cooperative learning, experiential learning, differentiated instruction, gamification
Advertisement Presentations Invite each group to present. Give each group 1 minute. Other groups use rubric to award points. Present their advertisement. Audience groups listen, observe and award points based on the rubric. Performance task, peer assessment, gamification
Consolidate/Closure

Ask groups to provide total scores for the other groups or provide group with highest score and explain why.

Consolidate and announce winner.

Congratulations to all groups.

Ask: What is an output device?

Name four output devices.

Give one characteristic of your group’s device.

Why might someone need your device?

 Give scores and reasons for their selection and reflect on what they learned Reflection, justification
Assessment

Formative Assessment

During the lesson, the teacher assesses students through:

  • Responses to the set-induction questions

  • Oral questioning

  • Identification of output devices

  • Observation of group participation

Performance Assessment

  • The advertisement is assessed using the 10-point peer rubric.

Summative Assessment

Students should be able to answer:

  • What is an output device?

  • Name four output devices.

  • Give one characteristic of your group’s device.

  • Why might someone need your device?
