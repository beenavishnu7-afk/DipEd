Purpose Statement

Why this portfolio was created and what it sets out to demonstrate.

According to the purpose and benefits of a teaching portfolio-Macpherson Institute (2025), “a teaching portfolio provides the opportunity to engage in meaningful reflection, compile evidence of your effectiveness as an educator, and craft the narrative around, your teaching journey while also serving as a critical resource for obtaining recognition of your effectiveness as an educator.”

As a teacher of Information Technology in a secondary school in Trinidad and Tobago my intention in developing this portfolio is to document my professional growth and reflect on how the Postgraduate Diploma in Education programme has influenced my teaching practice. I want to demonstrate how I develop lesson plans and unit plans, select appropriate assessment strategies, and justify my decisions to meet the learners’ needs.

This portfolio will showcase how I integrate technology into my lessons and connect Information Technology with other subject areas and real-life situations. I have learnt that students learn more meaningfully when they understand how the knowledge and skills acquired in the classroom can be applied beyond school. I also want to show how student-centered approaches to lesson planning and implementation is used to encourage participation, collaboration, critical thinking, and problem solving.

This portfolio will also show through reflection how this programme has strengthened my understanding of my discipline and encouraged me to move beyond just imparting knowledge to guiding students in their learning. It will also be used as a guide as I continue to develop my profession as an Information Technology teacher.

Letter to Self

August 10th, 2026.

My dearest self, Beena,

I was in secondary school when my uncle told me to apply to teach as it was a secure job and had good benefits for a lady, such as holidays and maternity leave benefits and the like. At the time, I just listened and nodded but did not give any serious thought to it, as I felt there were a lot more options. However, as it turned out, I truly believe now that teaching was my “calling”.

While waiting for results from Advanced level exams, I offered tuition for cxc mathematics to kids from the village. During this time, I was also offered the position to teach at the primary government school in my village for the Adult Education Unit, part of a government program, teaching mathematics for all levels of secondary school.

Although I had several other types of jobs, such as secretary for both a tint shop and two private schools as well as being a customer service representative at a bank, teaching always came naturally to me. I accepted teaching jobs at Trinizuela Technical and Vocational College, after which I entered the on-the-job training program for teaching and while waiting to be called for teaching I started working as a customer service representative at a bank. As faith would have it, I was hired by the Ministry of Education the same week I resigned from the bank. It has been twenty-six years thus far in this noble profession, and I never regretted the decision to become a teacher.

I chose this program for several reasons. Firstly, I hope that it will add to my knowledge in terms of teaching and learning philosophies and improve my teaching skills so that I can deliver lessons in an engaging, interactive and fun manner to captivate the attention of my students and to help them learn faster and retain more information not only for exam purposes but also for life. Secondly, I hope that it formalizes my knowledge and experience allowing me to have the recognized qualification for advancement in this career, if necessary. Also, this would be the last year that this government will be offering the program at no cost to teachers.

I hope that this program offers new insights into teaching methods, especially for the field of information technology. I hope to improve lesson planning skills to incorporate objectives from all aspects of blooms taxonomy into my lessons rather than just focusing on the cognitive. I wish to understand how students learn and how to plan and deliver lessons to cater to their needs. I expect that this program will build personal discipline as it requires a lot of time and dedication. I also hope that new professional relationships may be forged with fellow student-teachers while working together as a team to help each other to succeed.

Warm regards,

Beena Seepersad-Vishnu
