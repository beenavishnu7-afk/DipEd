B - Unit and Lesson Planning

Lesson plan 3: Spreadsheet functions

Using predefined spreadsheet functions.

Lesson Plan

Teacher Name:

Beena Seepersad-Vishnu

Subject:

Information Technology

Class Level:

Form 4

Unit:

Spreadsheet

 Lesson: Spreadsheet Functions Date:

Time 35 minutes

  1. Prior knowledge/skills

Students should be able to:

• Identify spreadsheet rows, columns, cells and cell ranges.

• Enter data and use basic formulas in Microsoft Excel.

• Recognize cell references and perform simple calculations.

Quick check: Ask students to identify cell B2 and explain what a range such as B2:B6 means.

  1. Lesson objectives :

At the end of this lesson students should be able to:

1) Given a function reference list, students will identify the purposes of at least 8 out of the 11 spreadsheet functions introduced. (Cognitive, Remember)

2) After the teacher’s demonstration, the students will accurately explain the difference between COUNT and COUNT A. (Cognitive, Understand)

3) State two benefits of predefined functions. (Cognitive, Understand)

4) Given five spreadsheet scenarios in Mentimeter, students will select the appropriate function for at least four out of the five scenarios. (Cognitive, Apply)

5) Given a prepared dataset, a worksheet and Microsoft Excel, students working in pairs will correctly construct and apply at least three spreadsheet functions, using correct syntax and cell ranges. (Psychomotor)

6) During the team challenge, students will contribute at least one answer or explanation, listen respectfully to their partners and cooperate in completing the assigned tasks. (Affective)

  1. Set induction

Display a spreadsheet containing sales figures from a school fundraising event. Ask: “The coordinator needs total sales, average sales, highest sales and the number of students who reached a target. Can we solve these problems efficiently without calculating everything manually?”

Introduce the mission: students are Spreadsheet Function Detectives who must crack the code by choosing and testing the correct functions.

  1. Resources including digital resources

  • Laptops

  • projector

  • Microsoft Excel

  • Mentimeter presentation (or printed response cards if unavailable)

  • Prepared spreadsheet dataset and Function Detective worksheet

  • Function reference sheet, function/scenario cards

  • Whiteboard and markers

  • Assessment checklist and exit tickets

  1. Content/ Teaching Points

1. Spreadsheet functions: predefined formulas that perform calculations or tasks efficiently and accurately.

2. Common functions and purposes

• SUM – adds values.

• AVERAGE – calculates the arithmetic mean.

• MAX / MIN – finds the highest / lowest value.

• COUNT – counts cells containing numbers.

COUNTA – counts non-empty cells.

• COUNTIF – counts cells that meet a condition.

• IF – returns one result if a condition is true and another if false.

• VLOOKUP – searches the first column of a table and returns a value from another column in the same row.

• PMT – calculates a periodic loan payment for a constant interest rate and payment schedule.

• DATE – creates a date from year, month and day values.

3. Example formulas

=SUM(B2:B6)

=AVERAGE(C2:C6)

=MAX(C2:C6)

=COUNTIF(C2:C6,">=50")

=IF(C2>=50,"Pass","Needs Improvement")

4. Key distinction: COUNT counts numeric entries; COUNTA counts all non-empty cells.

5. Gamification and scoring

Teams earn 2 points for a correct function choice, 2 for a sound justification, 2 for a correctly constructed formula and 1 for respectful teamwork/participation. No points are deducted for mistakes; errors are learning opportunities.

f. Teaching strategies and g. Student Activities

Teaching strategies

• Gamification: detective storyline, mission levels, points and recognition.

• Demonstration and guided questioning to model function use.

• Problem-based and experiential learning through realistic spreadsheet scenarios.

• Cooperative learning: students work in pairs as detective partners.

• Immediate feedback using Mentimeter results and teacher explanations.

• Differentiation:

– Support: function reference sheet, guided examples and partially completed formulas.

– Expected level: select, justify and apply functions with a partner.

– Extension: use VLOOKUP with a grading table or explore PMT.

• Contingency: if Mentimeter or individual devices are unavailable, display questions and use printed response cards; students can share laptops.

Student activities

1. Observe the fundraising spreadsheet and predict how the tasks could be completed.

2. Watch the demonstration of SUM, AVERAGE, MAX and MIN; identify the purpose of other functions from the reference sheet.

3. Complete five Mentimeter function scenarios with a partner and justify selected answers.

4. Use the sample dataset below to calculate total sales, average marks, highest mark, number of marks at least 50, and Pass/Needs Improvement classifications.

5. Test formulas in Excel, interpret results and troubleshoot errors.

6. Complete the final detective challenge and an exit ticket.

h. Sectional review/ Assessment

Diagnostic assessment

• Ask students to identify a cell and cell range and explain a basic formula.

Formative assessment

• Mentimeter: use five scenarios and check whether students select the correct function in at least four.

• Ask students to justify choices and explain the difference between COUNT and COUNTA.

• Observe formula syntax, range selection, interpretation of results, participation and teamwork using a checklist.

Mentimeter questions (answer key)

1. Total sales in B2:B10? SUM.

2. Average marks in C2:C20? AVERAGE.

3. Five cells contain three numbers, one text entry and one blank: COUNT = 3; COUNTA = 4.

4. Count marks at least 50 in D2:D25? COUNTIF; example: =COUNTIF(D2:D25,">=50").

5. Display “Pass” for a score of 50 or more, otherwise “Needs Improvement”? IF.

Practical assessment

Check whether pairs correctly construct and apply at least three functions in Excel and explain what their results mean.

Exit ticket

1. Which function finds the lowest mark?

2. Explain the difference between COUNT and COUNTA.

3. Which function counts students scoring below 40, and why?

4. State one benefit of predefined spreadsheet functions.

i. Closure

Review that spreadsheet functions help users complete calculations and solve problems efficiently and accurately. Ask students to name one function they can now use confidently and describe a real-world situation where it would be useful. Collect exit tickets and conclude: “Case closed—choose the function that matches the problem, construct it correctly, and check what the result tells you.”

j. Final evaluation/ Teacher Reflection

To be completed after the lesson:

1. Did gamification increase participation and motivation?

2. Could students justify their choices rather than guess?

3. Which functions caused the greatest difficulty, especially COUNT versus COUNTA?

4. Could students construct formulas with correct syntax and cell ranges and interpret their results?

5. Did competition encourage participation without discouraging students who needed support?

6. Which students need additional guidance or extension?

7. What went well, what did not go well, and what should be modified next time?

8. Are students ready to move on to more complex spreadsheet functions?
