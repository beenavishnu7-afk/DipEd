B - Unit and Lesson Planning

Lesson plan 1: Introduction to Spreadsheets

The first lesson of the Spreadsheet unit.

Lesson Plan

Teacher Name:

Beena Seepersad-Vishnu

Subject:

Information Technology

Class Level:

Form 4

Unit:

Spreadsheet

 Lesson: Introduction to Spreadsheets Date:

  1. Prior knowledge/skills

1. Students can identify rows and columns in a paper-based table.

2, Students have basic computer skills.

3, Students can follow instructions.

  1. Lesson objectives :

At the end of this lesson students should be able to:

1) Define a spreadsheet with 100% accuracy. (Cognitive, Remembering)

2) Explain the purpose of a spreadsheet as an electronic document used to capture, display, manipulate and calculate data arranged in rows and columns, with 100% accuracy. (Cognitive, Understanding)

3) Identify at least six of the following eight spreadsheet terms with 80% accuracy: row, column, cell, cell address, range, label, value and title. (Cognitive, Remembering)

4) Correctly label spreadsheet components, achieving at least 80% accuracy. (Cognitive, Applying)

5) Use Microsoft Excel to enter and organize labels and values in a spreadsheet, correctly identifying at least four spreadsheet components. (Psychomotor, Guided Response)

6) Participate willingly in class discussions and practical activities. (Affective, Responding)

7) Demonstrate cooperation by offering appropriate assistance to classmates and respecting their contributions. (Affective, Responding/Valuing)

  1. Set induction

1) The teacher distributes a paper-based table showing the number of lunches served to secondary school students in Trinidad and Tobago from 2015 to 2025.

2) Students examine the table and suggest how a computer could be used to organize and manipulate the information.

3) The teacher introduces Microsoft Excel as an application used to organize and manipulate data in a spreadsheet.

  1. Resources including digital resources

  • Computers with Microsoft Excel installed.

  • Projector and teacher's computer.

  • Whiteboard and markers.

  • Printed worksheet containing the lunch data for 2015–2025.

  • Sample completed spreadsheet.

  • Student activity sheet and spreadsheet terminology notes.

  1. Content/ Teaching Points

1) Definition and purpose of a spreadsheet.

2) Common spreadsheet terminology.

3) Identify spreadsheet components

4) Practical applications.

  1. Teaching strategies /g. student activities

  • Experiential learning: Students explore spreadsheet components using Microsoft Excel.

  • Demonstration: The teacher models how to identify and manipulate spreadsheet components.

  • Cooperative learning: Students provide appropriate assistance to peers during practical activities.

  • Questioning and discussion: Students explain the purpose of spreadsheets and identify examples of their use.

  • Guided practice: Students complete structured tasks with teacher support.

Differentiation

  • Provide individual assistance and step-by-step instructions for students who require additional support.

  • Use enlarged projected examples and printed reference notes for students who need visual guidance.

  • Provide additional identification and highlighting exercises for students who need more practice.

  • Allow students who complete the task early to identify additional cell addresses and ranges.

Contingency Plan

  • If a computer fails, students will share available computers.

  • If Microsoft Excel is unavailable, students will complete the identification and highlighting activities using the printed worksheet.

  • If the projector fails, the teacher will demonstrate using a working computer and printed examples.

HFLE Integration

Students will practice respectful listening, cooperation, confidence and responsible peer support. The teacher will reinforce the idea that mistakes are opportunities to learn and improve.

g. Student Activities

  1. Examine the printed table showing lunches served from 2015 to 2025.

  2. Participate in a class discussion about how spreadsheets can organize information.

  3. Observe the teacher's demonstration of spreadsheet terminology.

  4. Open Microsoft Excel and identify rows, columns, cells and cell addresses.

  5. Enter the title Lunches Served to Secondary School Students, 2015–2025.

  6. Enter the headings Year and Number of Lunches, followed by the supplied data.

  7. Highlight and identify a row, a column, a cell address and a range.

  8. Identify examples of labels, values and titles in the worksheet.

  9. Assist classmates respectfully when necessary.

  10. Save the completed spreadsheet using their names.

Oral Questioning

The teacher asks students the following questions:

  1. What is a spreadsheet?

  2. State two purposes of a spreadsheet.

  3. What is the difference between a row and a column?

  4. What is a cell?

  5. How is a cell address identified?

  6. What is a range? Give an example.

  7. Differentiate between a label and a value.

  8. What is the purpose of a title in a spreadsheet?

2. Practical Assessment

Students use their Excel worksheet to:

  • Identify one row and one column.

  • Identify two cell addresses.

  • Highlight one range of five cells.

  • Identify one label, one value and the title of the worksheet.

Assessment criterion: Students should correctly complete at least 80% of the identification tasks.

3. Exit Ticket: Multiple-Choice Questions

Question 1: What is a spreadsheet?

A. A program used only for typing letters.

B. A group or selection of cells, such as A1:A5.

C. An electronic document containing data arranged in rows and columns that can be manipulated and used in calculations.

D. A program used only to draw pictures.

Correct answer: C

Question 2: Which of the following is a purpose of a spreadsheet?

A. To organize, display and manipulate data.

B. To browse the internet only.

C. To produce charts only.

D. To type letters only.

Correct answer: A

Question 3: What is the cell address of the cell in column C, row 4?

A. 4C

B. C4

C. C3

D. D4

Correct answer: B

Question 4: What is a range?

A. A single column heading.

B. A group or selection of cells.

C. A spreadsheet title.

D. A single cell address.

Correct answer: B

Question 5: Which of the following is an example of a label?

A. The descriptive text Year entered into a cell.

B. The number 2025 entered into a cell.

C. The cell address B3.

D. The range A1:A5.

Correct answer: A

4. Assessment/Homework

Students will create a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet titled My Weekly Expenses.

The spreadsheet must include:

  1. A suitable title.

  2. Five expense items, such as transportation, lunch, stationery, phone credit and snacks.

  3. Appropriate labels and numerical values.

  4. Identification of the following:

    • One row.

    • One column.

    • Two cell addresses.

    • One range from the spreadsheet.

h. Closure

The teacher summarizes the lesson by reinforcing that a spreadsheet organizes data in rows and columns and can be used to display, manipulate and calculate information. Students should leave the lesson able to recognize common spreadsheet components and explain how they are used.

The teacher asks students to complete the following statement orally:

“One new thing I learned about spreadsheets today is…”

i. Final evaluation/ Teacher Reflection

To what extent did students meet the cognitive, affective and psychomotor objectives? Use practical work and exit tickets as evidence.

Did students participate in the discussion and demonstrate cooperation during practical activities?

Which terms or practical tasks did students find difficult? Were there any technical issues?

Did students who required additional assistance receive adequate support?

Should more time be allocated to identifying cell addresses and ranges?

Would additional demonstrations or peer-supported activities improve understanding?

Can students correctly identify spreadsheet components and enter data independently?
