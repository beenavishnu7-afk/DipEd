B - Unit and Lesson Planning

Lesson plan 5: Manipulating and formatting data

Formatting, sorting and filtering data.

Lesson Plan

Teacher Name:

Beena Seepersad-Vishnu

Subject:

Information Technology

Class Level:

Form 4

Unit:

Spreadsheet

Lesson: Manipulating

and Formatting Data

 Date:

  1. Prior knowledge/skills

Ask students:

• What is a spreadsheet used for?

• What is a cell, row and column?

• Demonstrate selecting a cell and entering a value.

Expected response: Students can identify basic spreadsheet parts and enter data.

  1. Lesson objectives :

At the end of this lesson students should be able to:

1) Identify at least three spreadsheet formatting tools accurately when presented with a spreadsheet and a list of formatting tools. (Cognitive, Remembering)

2) Explain accurately the purpose of sorting and filtering. (Cognitive, Understanding)

3) Format a small dataset and correctly sort and filter it, completing at least 4 of 5 task requirements. (Psychomotor)

4) Demonstrate responsibility and cooperation by checking their own work and giving a peer respectful feedback. (Affective)

  1. Set induction

Display a messy class fundraiser sales table with inconsistent headings, mixed number formats and unsorted entries. Ask: “If you had to find the highest sale or show only entries for one product, how would you make this table easier to read?” Invite two quick suggestions. Explain that students will act as data assistants and improve the table.

  1. Resources including digital resources

  • Laptop

  • Microsoft Excel

  • Prepared spreadsheet dataset (e.g., school fundraiser sales)

  • Data manipulation worksheet / task cards

  • Assessment checklist and peer-assessment checklist

  • Printed dataset as a contingency if devices or electricity are unavailable

  1. Content/ Teaching Points

Spreadsheet formatting: bold headings; adjust column width; apply borders/shading; format numbers as currency or suitable number formats.

2. Sorting: arrange records in ascending or descending order using a selected field (e.g., Sales from largest to smallest).

3. Filtering: display only records that meet selected criteria (e.g., Product = Juice).

4. Data accuracy: select the correct range and field; check that records remain together and values are not changed accidentally.

5. Responsible practice: review the result and undo/correct errors where necessary.

f. Teaching strategies and g. Student Activities

• Demonstration/illustration using the projected spreadsheet.

• Guided questioning to activate prior knowledge and check understanding.

• Discussion of why organised data is useful.

• Activity-based learning: students manipulate a real-life dataset.

• Cooperative learning through paired checking and feedback.

• Observation of student performance and individual guidance where necessary.

• Differentiation: provide step-by-step prompts for students who need support; extension task for students who finish early.

• Contingency: use a printed dataset and sorting/filtering scenario if computers are unavailable.

1. Observe the teacher’s demonstration and answer guided questions.

2. Open the prepared sales spreadsheet and save a working copy.

3. Format the title/headings, adjust column widths, add borders and apply a suitable number format.

4. Sort the sales records from highest to lowest.

5. Filter the table to show only one selected product.

6. Check the result with a partner using the checklist and correct any errors.

7. Explain one way formatting, sorting or filtering helps a user understand data.

h. Sectional review/ Assessment

During the lesson, ask:

1. What is one difference between sorting and filtering?

2. Which formatting tool would make headings stand out?

3. Why must you select the correct data range before sorting?

Practical assessment checklist (1 mark each):

□ Headings are clearly formatted.

□ Column widths / borders make the table readable.

□ Appropriate number format is applied.

□ Records are sorted correctly by Sales, largest to smallest.

□ Filter displays only the selected product.

Total: 5 marks. Success criterion: at least 4/5.

Use teacher observation and peer assessment to identify students needing further practice.

i. Closure

Ask students to complete the sentence: “Formatting helps a spreadsheet by…, sorting helps by…, and filtering helps by…”. Reinforce that formatting improves readability, sorting changes the order of records, and filtering displays only records that meet chosen criteria. Remind students to check data accuracy after every change.

j. Final evaluation/ Teacher Reflection

To be completed after the lesson:

• Were students able to format, sort and filter the dataset accurately?

• How many students achieved at least 4 out of 5 checklist items?

• Which task caused the most difficulty, and what misconceptions were observed?

• Did the demonstration and paired checking support student learning?

• What should be retaught or modified in the next lesson?

• Are students ready to apply these skills to a larger dataset?
