B - Unit and Lesson Planning

Unit plan report: Spreadsheet

The Form 4 Information Technology unit plan report: eight lessons over two weeks.

UNIT PLAN REPORT – FORM 4 INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY

Unit: Spreadsheet

UNIT PLAN ELEMENT FORM 4 INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY – REPORT DETAILS
Subject Information Technology
Level Form 4
Title Spreadsheet
Duration 2 weeks – 8 lessons
Suggested Lesson Duration 30 minutes per lesson
National Philosophy, Goals/Aims/General Objectives In today’s modern digital society, technology continues to shape the world in which we live. Microsoft Excel provides practical ways to enter, manipulate, organize, analyse and present data to support decision-making. Spreadsheet skills are valuable in education, everyday life and many careers. This unit develops students’ confidence in using spreadsheets to solve practical problems and make evidence-based decisions.
Knowledge – Formal Students should have basic computer skills and an understanding of numbers and simple mathematical operations. They should be able to follow instructions, identify information in a table, and perform basic calculations.
Knowledge – Informal Students may have used calculators, mobile applications, tables, budgets or simple spreadsheets. They may understand everyday tasks such as comparing prices, organizing information, calculating totals and interpreting charts.
Content/Concepts Spreadsheet purpose and terminology; rows, columns, cells, cell addresses, ranges, labels, values and titles; data entry and editing; arithmetic operators and brackets; relative and absolute cell references; copying formulae; predefined functions including SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT, COUNTA, COUNTIF, VLOOKUP, PMT, IF and DATE; formatting, inserting, deleting, sorting and filtering data; chart types, chart selection and labelling.
Relationships Data entry and organization → formulae and functions → data manipulation, sorting and filtering → chart creation and interpretation → evidence-based decisions. Students progress from understanding spreadsheet structure to applying spreadsheet tools to solve real-world problems.
Integration Across Curriculum

Mathematics: use formulae and functions to calculate totals, averages, percentages and comparisons.
Business Studies: prepare budgets, calculate revenue, expenses, profit/loss, discounts and VAT.
Science: organize experimental data and represent results using charts.
English Language: write clear headings, labels and brief interpretations of spreadsheet results.

Physical Education: plan a budget for a sports day using equipment costs, revenue from sales, calculate profit/loss
Lessons Lesson 1: Introduction to Spreadsheets – purpose, terminology, rows, columns, cells, cell addresses, ranges, labels, values and titles.
Lesson 2: Creating Spreadsheet Formulae – arithmetic operators, brackets, relative and absolute addressing, copying and applying formulae.
Lesson 3: Spreadsheet Functions – purpose and use of predefined functions; selecting suitable functions and interpreting results.
Lesson 4: Practice Lesson – apply spreadsheet formulae and functions to practical tasks.
Lesson 5: Manipulating and Formatting Data – format data; insert, delete and modify data; sort and filter using appropriate criteria.
Lesson 6: Performing Chart Operations – select chart types, create charts, add titles and labels, and justify chart choices.
Lesson 7: Practice Lesson on Chart Operations – create and label charts from given data and evaluate presentation.
Lesson 8: Unit Evaluation – complete an integrated practical task using spreadsheet skills developed throughout the unit.
Resources Desktop computers or laptops; Microsoft Excel or a compatible spreadsheet application; projector or smart display; prepared spreadsheet datasets; sample spreadsheets; formula and function reference sheets; terminology and task cards; worksheets; assessment checklists; whiteboard and markers; notebooks and stationery.
Teaching Strategies Direct instruction; teacher demonstration and modelling; guided questioning; discussion and brainstorming; inquiry-based learning; experiential, hands-on learning; problem-based learning; cooperative learning and peer tutoring; independent practice; differentiated instruction; practical challenges and gamification where appropriate.
Assessment Plan/Strategies Diagnostic assessment: questioning and discussion to check prior knowledge of tables, basic calculations, computer use and spreadsheet terminology.
Formative assessment: observation of practical work; oral questioning; terminology matching; formula prediction; function selection; error identification; checks of formatting, sorting, filtering and chart labelling; peer assessment; worksheets and exit tickets.
Practical assessment: students enter and organize data, apply appropriate formulae and functions, manipulate and format a dataset, sort and filter records, create and label a suitable chart, and explain what the results show.
Summative assessment: an integrated spreadsheet task assessing the main skills taught across the eight lessons, using a checklist or rubric for accuracy, appropriate tool selection, presentation and interpretation.
Teacher’s Reflection After each lesson consider:
Were the objectives met?
Which concepts or spreadsheet skills required additional support?
Was sufficient time provided for practical activities?
Were the teaching strategies effective in supporting student engagement and understanding?
Did students work accurately and responsibly with data?
What were the strengths of the lesson?
What changes or additional scaffolding are needed?

At the end of the unit consider:
To what extent could students independently enter and organize data, create formulae and functions, manipulate data, and create and interpret charts?
Could students apply spreadsheet skills to practical, real-world situations and use the results to make evidence-based decisions?
