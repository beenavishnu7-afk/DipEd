B - Unit and Lesson Planning

Unit plan: Spreadsheet

The unit plan for Form 4 Information Technology: rationale, unit goals and objectives, and the eight lessons with their objectives, strategies, student engagement, approaches, assessment and resources.

Topic : SpreadsheetsCalss : Form 4Durstion: 30 minutes per lessonNumber of Lessons: 8

Rationale

In today’s modern digital society, technology continues to shape the world in which we live. Microsoft Excel is one tool that allows for pratical ways to work with, manipulate, organize and present data to support the decision making process. In many careers today, this skill is valued by many employers whether someone is now starting out or aiming to assume new resposibilities. Microsoft Excel can open new pathways to new and improved opportunities.

Unit Goal

The goals of this unit are:

[1] Data entry and organization.

[2] Develop proficiency in creating and applying formulae in order to save time.

[3] Analyze, filter and process data to determine trends using worksheets.

[4] Present data to illustrate trends using charts and graphs.

[5] Make evidenced based decisions.

Unit Objectives

At the end of this unit students will:

[1] Explain the purpose of a spreadsheet

[2] Use appropriate terminologies and notions commonly associated with spreadsheets

[3] Manipulate columns and rows

[4] Manipulate data in a spreadsheet

[5] Use basic pre-defined systems functions

[6] Create advanced arithmetic formulae

[7] Replicate (copy) formulae into other cells

[8] Manipulate columns, rows and data in a spreadsheet

[9] Perform charting operations

1 Introduction to Spreadsheets

Students will:

[1] define ‘a spreadsheet’

with 100% accuracy [based on cxc syllabus]

[2] state its purpose: captures, displays and manipulates data in a tabula format with 100% accuracy.

[3] identify by highlighting, and define: row, column, cell, cell address, range, label, value and title.

[4] use/manipulate common spreadsheet terminology with at least 80% accuracy. [row, column, cell, cell address, range, label, value and title.]

[5] display willingness to participate in class activities.

[6] volunteer assistance to fellow students in the class.

[1] Demonstration and Discussion:

- Teacher displays a sample of a completed spreadsheet and questions students on what they observe and writes main points on the board.

- Teacher questions students on what they think its purpose is.

[2] Direct Instruction:

- Teacher illustrates how and instructs students to open the Excel programme.

[3] Demonstration and Instruction:

- Teacher identifies and manipulate the following and instructs students to do same: row, column, cell, cell address, range, label, value and title.

[4] Activity Based Learning

[1] Student share prior knowledge and ideas.

[2] Listen and respond to questions and provide examples.

[3] Identify features on the displayed spreadsheet.

[4] Work in pairs to classify examples.

[5] Perform the tasks and identify the selected cells/ranges.

[6] Complete the exit ticket.

3 Spreadsheet Functions

[1] Explain the purpose of predefined spreadsheet functions in simplifying and automating calculations.

[2] Classify the functions SUM, AVERAGE, MAX, MIN, COUNT, COUNTA, COUNTIF, VLOOKUP, PMT, IF and DATE, according to the type of task they perform.

[3] Select the most appropriate function for a given spreadsheet problem.

[4] Interpret the results produced by selected spreadsheet functions in a real-world context.

[5] Differentiate between functions that perform similar tasks, such as COUNT AND COUNTA.

[6] Work cooperatively and responsibly with peers while solving spreadsheet-based problems.

[7] Demonstrate confidence in using spreadsheet functions to solve given problems.

[8] Construct and apply functions to solve problems.

6 Performing Chart Operations

[5] Prepare the various charts.

[1] Demonstration on choosing chart type and labelling.

[2] Activity Based Learning

[3] Oral questioning.

[4] Class discussion.

[5] Brainstorming.

[1] analyze it and select the appropriate chart type.

[2] create the chart.

[3] label the chart accordingly.

[4] illustrate the same data by creating other chart types.

[5] evaluate which chart type is appropriate to represent the data.

[6] justify their choice.
