B - Unit and Lesson Planning

Lesson plan 2: Spreadsheet formula

Creating spreadsheet formulae with arithmetic operators and brackets.

Lesson Plan

Teacher Name:

Beena Seepersad-Vishnu

Subject:

Information Technology

Class Level:

Form 4

Unit:

Spreadsheet

Lesson:

Spreadsheet Formula

 Date:

  1. Prior knowledge/skills

Ask “What information would an employer need to calculate an employee’s pay?”

Expected responses include hours worked, hourly rate, gross pay, deductions and net pay.

Students should be able to enter data into spreadsheet cells, identify rows and columns, and navigate a worksheet.

  1. Lesson objectives :

At the end of this lesson students should be able to:

1) Explain the purpose of arithmetic operators (+, −, *, /) and brackets in spreadsheet formulae. (Cognitive, Understand)

2) Differentiate between relative addressing (e.g., D3) and absolute addressing (e.g., $J$2). (Cognitive, Analyze)

3) Predict the effect of copying, moving and deleting cells containing formulae on resulting calculations. (Cognitive, Analyze)

4) Determine correct formulae using addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and brackets to solve problems. (Cognitive, Apply)

5) Participate actively in practical activities involving the creation and replication of formulae. (Affective)

6) Cooperate with peers in practical activities involving the creation and replication of formulae. (Affective)

7) Construct spreadsheet formulae using arithmetic operators and brackets to solve a real-world payroll problem. (Psychomotor)

  1. Set induction

“Payroll Mystery: Where Did the Money Go?”

Present the scenario: “Cherry worked 40 hours and earns $12.50 per hour. Her employer deducts 5% tax. Can you calculate her gross pay, tax and take-home pay before the payroll system does?”

Students discuss their estimates with a partner for 30 seconds. Invite two pairs to share their methods. Reveal: gross pay = $500; tax = $25; net pay = $475.

Ask: “If a company has 100 employees, should the payroll clerk calculate every person’s pay by hand?” Use responses to introduce the value of spreadsheet formulae that can be copied down a table.

  1. Resources including digital resources

My Payroll.xlsx workbook; laptops with Excel; smart TV; whiteboard and markers; short formula task sheet.

  1. Content/ Teaching Points

Spreadsheet formulae begin with = and use cell references, numbers and arithmetic operators.

Arithmetic operators: + addition; − subtraction; * multiplication; / division. Brackets group operations and help ensure that calculations follow the intended order.

Using the payroll worksheet and row 3 as an example: Gross Pay in F3: =D3*E3. Tax in G3: =F3*5%. Net Pay in H3: =F3-G3. Copy the formulae down for other employee rows.

Relative addressing: a reference such as D3 changes relative to the destination when a formula is copied. For example, =D3*E3 copied one row down becomes =D4*E4.

Absolute addressing: $J$2 keeps the column and row fixed when copied. As an extension, enter 5% in J2 and use =F3*$J$2 for tax.

Copying formulae normally adjusts relative references while absolute references remain fixed. Moving formulae may affect references depending on the operation. Deleting a referenced cell may cause an incorrect result or an error such as #REF!.

Check that gross pay equals hours worked multiplied by rate per hour, tax is 5% of gross pay, and net pay equals gross pay minus tax.

  1. Teaching strategies /g. student activities

1) Demonstration/Illustration — model entering the payroll formulae and copying them down the worksheet.

2) Guided Questioning — ask students to predict results and explain the operators and cell references selected.

3) Discussion — students discuss how formulae calculate gross pay, tax and net pay and why accuracy matters.

4) Observation of students — monitor participation, formula entry, replication and troubleshooting during the practical activity.

5) Individual guidance where necessary — provide prompts, formula scaffolds or one-to-one assistance for students who need support.

6) Activity-Based Learning — students actively create and replicate formulae using the payroll worksheet.

Differentiated Instruction

  • Provide a step-by-step instruction sheet, a partially completed spreadsheet, and individual guidance where necessary.

  • Students will create and label a chart using the supplied dataset and follow the stated requirements.

  • Students will create a second chart using the same dataset, compare the two chart types, and justify which chart communicates the information more effectively.

Contingency Plan
If technical difficulties arise, the teacher will use a demonstration spreadsheet projected on the screen and guide students through the chart operations verbally. If a computer or Excel application is unavailable, students may identify suitable chart types and label the required chart components using a printed dataset and chart template. If students experience difficulty, the teacher will provide an additional worked example before they resume independent practice.

HFLE

Students will practice cooperation, respect, and constructive feedback when reviewing a peer’s chart.

h. Sectional review/ Assessment

Formative assessment: observe students during the practical task, question them about their formula choices, and check that formulae are entered and copied accurately.

Practical success criteria: students enter or identify the gross pay, tax and net pay formulae; replicate them down the employee rows; explain a relative and an absolute reference; and predict one effect of copying, moving or deleting a referenced cell.

Individual exit ticket:

1. Write the formula for gross pay if hours are in D4 and the rate per hour is in E4.

2. If gross pay is in F4, write the formula to calculate 5% tax.

3. If the tax rate is stored in J2, which formula keeps the tax-rate reference fixed when copied down: =F4*J2 or =F4*$J$2? Explain.

4. What happens to =D3*E3 when copied one row down? What may happen if a referenced cell is deleted?

Expected answers: =D4*E4; =F4*5%; =F4*$J$2 because it is an absolute reference; copying down changes the first formula to =D4*E4; deleting a referenced cell may cause an error such as #REF! or affect the result.

  1. Closure

Emphasize that formulae reduce repetitive calculations, but accurate results depend on selecting the correct operators, using brackets appropriately and understanding cell references.

Ask students to complete: “When I copy a formula down the payroll table, I must remember that…” Expected response: relative references usually change, while absolute references remain fixed.

  1. Final evaluation/ Teacher Reflection

To what extent did students meet the cognitive, affective and psychomotor objectives? Use practical work and exit tickets as evidence.

Which students entered and replicated formulae accurately? Who needs more practice with brackets, relative references or absolute references?

Did the Payroll Mystery engage students and connect the lesson to real life? Was the 35-minute timing appropriate?

What went well, what misconceptions arose, and what would be changed next time? Are students ready to progress to more complex formulae?
