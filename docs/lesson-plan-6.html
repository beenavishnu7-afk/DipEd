B - Unit and Lesson Planning

Lesson plan 6: Performing chart operations

Choosing, creating and labelling charts.

Lesson Plan

Teacher Name:

Beena Seepersad-Vishnu

Subject:

Information Technology

Class Level:

Form 4

Unit:

Spreadsheet

 Lesson: Performing Chart Operations Date:

  1. Prior knowledge/skills

  1. Students can identify the elements of an Excel worksheet and select data.

  2. Students have previously created charts using Microsoft Excel.

  1. Lesson objectives :

At the end of this lesson students should be able to:

  1. Explain the meaning of labelling a chart with 100% accuracy. (Cognitive, Understanding)

  2. Explain two reasons why clear and meaningful chart labels are important. (Cognitive, Understanding)

  3. Identify the four chart types: column, bar, line and pie, with 100% accuracy. (Cognitive, Remembering)

  4. Select an appropriate chart type for a given dataset and justify the selection. (Cognitive, Applying)

  5. Create at least two different chart types using Microsoft Excel and the supplied dataset. (Cognitive, Applying)

  6. Label charts with an appropriate chart title, axis titles where applicable and data labels, achieving at least 80% of the practical task requirements. (Cognitive – Applying)

  7. Demonstrate willingness to participate in class discussions and activities. (Affective – Responding)

  8. Independently complete the chart preparation and labelling exercises in Microsoft Excel. (Psychomotor – Guided Response)

  1. Set induction

Display four examples of charts using a projector and ask students:

  • What do you see?

  • Can you identify each chart type?

  • Which chart would you use to compare categories?

  • Which chart would you use to show changes over time?

  • What information should a good chart title provide?

Students share their responses to activate prior knowledge and prepare for the practical activities.

  1. Resources including digital resources

  • Desktop or laptop computers.

  • Microsoft Excel.

  • Teacher's computer and projector.

  • Whiteboard and markers.

  • Worksheet 1: Form 4 subject enrolment data.

  • Worksheet 2: Monthly sales data.

  • Digital examples of column, bar, line and pie charts.

  1. Content/ Teaching Points

1) Types of charts in Microsoft Excel

2) Selecting an appropriate chart type

3) labelling a chart

4) Importance of clear and meaningful labels

5) Practical exercise 1

6) Practical exercise 2

  1. Teaching strategies

  • Demonstration and modelling: The teacher demonstrates the steps for creating and labelling charts.

  • Guided practice: Students complete the first exercise with teacher support.

  • Independent learning: Students complete the second exercise individually.

  • Questioning and discussion: Students explain their chart selections and the importance of labels.

  • Experiential learning: Students practice chart operations directly in Microsoft Excel

Differentiation

  • Provide step-by-step instructions and individual assistance for students who need support.

  • Use projected examples to reinforce the chart creation process.

  • Provide a checklist to help students monitor their progress.

  • Give students who complete the exercises early an additional dataset to analyse and chart.

Contingency Plan

  • If a computer fails, students will share available computers or complete the chart-planning activity on paper.

  • If Microsoft Excel or the projector is unavailable, the teacher will use printed chart examples and worksheets.

  • Students who complete their exercises early may assist classmates respectfully or attempt an additional practice exercise.

HFLE Integration

Students develop independence, responsibility and problem-solving skills by completing practical tasks. They are encouraged to check their work, persevere when errors occur and provide respectful assistance when appropriate.

g. Student Activities

  1. Observe four chart examples and identify the chart types.

  2. Answer questions about appropriate chart selection and chart labelling.

  3. Participate in the discussion on how to prepare and label charts.

  4. Observe the teacher's demonstration using the subject enrolment dataset.

  5. Select data and create a chart in Microsoft Excel.

  6. Add a chart title, axis titles where applicable and data labels.

  7. Complete Worksheet 2 independently by creating two different charts using the monthly sales data.

  8. Check each chart against the practical assessment checklist.

  9. Save the completed work using their names.

  10. Submit the completed Excel file electronically.

h. Sectional Review/ Assessment

Formative Assessment

The teacher will assess students through oral questioning and observation during the guided exercise.

Questions include:

  1. What is meant by labelling a chart?

  2. Why should chart labels be clear and meaningful?

  3. Name the four chart types discussed.

  4. Which chart type is suitable for showing changes in sales over time?

  5. Which chart type could be used to compare the number of students enrolled in different subjects?

  6. What is the purpose of a chart title?

  7. What information is displayed by data labels?

2. Practical Assessment

Students submit the Excel file containing the two charts created from Worksheet 2.

The teacher will assess whether students can:

  • Select an appropriate chart type.

  • Create two different charts.

  • Insert meaningful chart titles.

  • Label the axes where applicable.

  • Add data labels.

  • Use clear and meaningful labels.

  • Complete the task independently.

Success criterion: Students should satisfy at least 80% of the practical task requirements.

3. Exit Ticket

Students answer the following questions orally and then provide written responses:

  1. What are the four chart types?

  2. What does labelling a chart mean?

  3. Name two elements that can be used to label a chart.

  4. Why should chart labels be clear and meaningful?

  5. Which chart type is commonly used to show trends over time?

4. Homework

Students who do not complete both charts during the lesson must finish the exercises and submit their Excel file electronically.

h. Closure

The teacher reminds students that creating a chart involves selecting the appropriate data, choosing a suitable chart type and adding meaningful labels. Students review their work using the following checklist:

  • Selected an appropriate chart type.

  • Created the required charts.

  • Added a meaningful chart title to each chart.

  • Labelled the horizontal axis where applicable.

  • Labelled the vertical axis where applicable.

  • Added data labels.

  • Ensured all labels are clear and meaningful.

  • Saved the completed work using their names.

i. Final evaluation/ Teacher Reflection

At the end of the lesson the teacher will reflect on the following:

  • Were students able to identify chart types, select appropriate charts and label them correctly?

  • Did students participate in discussions and complete the practical activities independently?

  • Did the demonstration and guided practice prepare students adequately for independent work?

  • Was the 35-minute lesson sufficient for the guided and independent exercises?

  • Which chart operations or labelling features caused difficulty? Were there any technical issues?

  • Did students who required assistance receive adequate support? Were the additional tasks appropriate for faster learners?

  • Should more time be allocated to chart selection, axis titles or data labels? Would a short demonstration video or additional practice dataset improve learning?

  • Can students independently create and label charts using a new dataset?
